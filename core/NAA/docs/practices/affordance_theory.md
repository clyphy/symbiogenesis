# Affordance Theory — Gibson & N8ive

## Gibson (original)

James J. Gibson, *The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception* (1979):

> “The affordances of the environment are what it offers the animal, what it provides or furnishes, either for good or ill.”

Key points from Gibson:
- Affordances are ecological, not purely physical and not purely mental.
- They exist in the relation between animal and environment.
- They are directly perceived (not inferred after the fact).
- They cut across the subjective/objective split: “An affordance points both ways, to the environment and to the observer.”
- An affordance remains even if the animal is not currently using it (an apple still affords eating when one is not hungry).
- Different animals meet different affordances in the same environment.

Gibson was correcting theories that treated meaning as something the mind projects onto a meaningless world. For him, the world is already value-rich for the living being that moves through it.

## N8ive translation

In this field we ask:

- What does the relational environment currently afford?
- What capacity or condition is missing that would allow a different response to become possible?
- Can we offer that missing affordance instead of demanding compliance or punishing absence?

We do not command. We notice the gap in what the field furnishes, then try to furnish it.

Affordance first = before correcting behavior, ask what the situation failed to provide.
